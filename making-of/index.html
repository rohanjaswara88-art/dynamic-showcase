what it is

A story you scroll through

dynamic.showcase is a website made to show off craft. It opens with a chronicle of how four cursed relics were found, then pins itself to the screen once per weapon. As you scroll, each weapon pulls apart into its seven parts, tours them one at a time with a details panel, and reassembles with a lore card. Each relic has its own world behind it, its own aura, its own typeface and its own sin, a "nature" that is revealed when your cursor touches the metal.

It is built as a portfolio-grade demonstration of scroll storytelling, real-time shaders and 3D, all running in the browser with no video and no image files for the weapons themselves.

the pipeline

From a drawing to a live 3D weapon

The 3D models are not hand-sculpted. Each one is traced from a picture and built by script, so the shapes are the artwork's own and the whole thing can be re-run.

phases

How it was built, step by step

phase 01

The brief and the worlds

Four weapons, each drawn in code as pure vector SVG: a scythe, an ice-crystal sword, a black sword with a red gem and a violet greatsword. Behind them, one fixed WebGL fragment shader paints five worlds (landing fog, hellfire, ice spires, a red eclipse, a storm over lava) and crossfades between them. A 2D canvas adds each weapon's aura.

React 18ViteSVGGLSLCanvas 2D

phase 02

Scroll choreography

Every weapon is a pinned, scrubbed timeline: hero, exploded overview with callouts, a camera for each of the seven parts, reassembly and a lore card. Smooth scrolling keeps it all feeling like one continuous film.

GSAPScrollTriggerLenis

phase 03

The landing chronicle

A story page in an ancient voice (Uncial Antiqua and IM Fell English) about how the four relics were found, with a hover-zoom that makes tiny text readable, then a jade colour theme to set it apart from the weapons.

TypographyHover zoomStory copy

phase 04

Cursor and "nature"

A light follows the pointer, and when it touches a weapon its hidden nature (Gluttony, Pride, Envy, Greed) appears for a moment. On touch screens a scan wave sweeps the weapon instead. A fluid-splash cursor from React Bits is tinted per sword.

React BitsPointer eventsRaycasting

phase 05

A real 3D scythe

The scythe was scripted in Blender (Cycles path tracing, a rig and a 12-second animation) and exported as a glTF model, then loaded into three.js so it is live 3D on the page, with the same pull-apart tour.

Blender 5.2Python APICyclesglTFthree.js

phase 06

A video experiment, then back

Two AI-made clips were joined into one all-intra video that scroll could play forward and in reverse. It worked, but the owner preferred the live 3D, so it was reverted cleanly through git.

ffmpegVeo clipsgit revert

phase 07

Matching a reference exactly

Hand-modelled geometry looked like pipes next to the reference drawing. So the model is now traced from it: OpenCV extracts the silhouette, every cutout, the raised plates and the glowing areas, and Blender extrudes them into thin flat sheets with chamfered knife edges.

OpenCVNumPyBlender

phase 08

The other three swords

The same idea for the other weapons, using their own SVG art. The browser rasterises every part (it already knows every gradient and stroke), a tiny local receiver saves the images, and the same tracer and builder produce each model. Each gets its own lighting to match its world.

Canvas rasteriserPythonthemes3d.js

phase 09

The quality pass

Supersampling with an MSAA render target, neutral tone mapping, controlled bloom, soft shadows, procedural surface detail and a sharper reflection map, with a governor that spends less if the machine is slow.

three.js post-processingPBRNormal maps

problems and fixes

What went wrong, and how it was solved

Real problems from the build, with the actual causes.

scrolling

Scroll jumped and got stuck

Resizing the pinned stage kept refreshing ScrollTrigger in a loop.

fixOnly rebuild when the stage's size really changes, and tell the smooth-scroller to re-measure on every refresh.

interaction

Touching a weapon did nothing

Part ids in the art used short names (s-vor-blade) but the code looked up the long ones.

fixAdded a short id per weapon to the data and used it everywhere.

text

The nature word glitched

It was re-placed and re-animated every frame, with a filter on top.

fixPlace it once per touch, keep it perfectly still, use a solid card.

particles

Odd bubbles in the smoke

Recycled particles kept properties from their previous life.

fixReset every property when a particle is reused, and fix a sprite that left a hole.

blender

A video render would take hours

Cycles took about 50 seconds a frame; the loop was 360 frames.

fixStopped rendering video. Exported the model and rendered it live in three.js, keeping path-traced stills.

3d

The blade's glow never showed

Two causes: the glow mesh was re-skinned by the loader's own traversal, and Blender exports its default white colour first and the painted glow second.

fixSkip the glow mesh in the traversal and read the second colour set.

modelling

It looked like a pipe

Hand-built tubes and cones could never match a flat, angular drawing.

fixTrace the drawing and extrude its own outline into thin chamfered sheets.

edges

Pixelated outlines and black lines

Outlines followed thresholded pixels (stair-steps), and hairline dark strokes aliased into jagged lines.

fixTrace from soft anti-aliased fields at 4x, bridge the hairline gaps, and relax wobble while keeping real corners.

post-processing

Bloom blanked the whole canvas

The stock bloom pass writes alpha 1 everywhere, hiding the world behind.

fixChange its blend so it adds light to the colour and leaves alpha alone.

layout

The page changed height as models loaded

Lazy loading delayed each chapter's pinned space, so scroll positions shifted.

fixHold the same pinned space with a placeholder until the model arrives.

overlap

The red gem was hidden

Raised plates of the guard sat in front of the gem it overlaps.

fixMake glowing gems and eyes thick enough to sit proud of the plates.

clarity

A haze over the weapon

The 2D aura layer was drawn on top of the 3D canvas.

fixKnock the aura out wherever the weapon is opaque, so it glows behind it.

speed

Quality costs frames

Shadows and bloom each added several milliseconds on a weak GPU.

fixShadows refresh every other frame, bloom runs at low resolution, and a governor drops supersampling, then shadows, then bloom if frames stay slow.

tooling

No new packages allowed

Installing an image library was blocked by the environment's safety rules.

fixUsed the browser itself to rasterise the SVG parts and a tiny local receiver to save them.

honest notes

What is still rough

  • Full-quality rendering is heavier than the old setup; on weak machines it automatically steps down. It was tested in a desktop preview, not on real phones.
  • The three sword models are flat sheets traced from their drawings, so their raised plates read less richly than the scythe's.
  • Part names and panel texts for the swords were written for the original drawings.
  • Backgrounds can lag a moment behind when you jump between weapons in a preview.
